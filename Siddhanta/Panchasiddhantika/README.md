## About the Text

The *Pañcasiddhāntikā* ("Treatise on the Five [Astronomical] Canons") by Varāhamihira is the most important historical document for pre-medieval Indian astronomy. Written in the 6th century CE, it is the sole surviving source that summarizes the doctrines of five ancient astronomical schools (*siddhāntas*), four of which are otherwise lost:

1. **Paitāmaha Siddhānta** — the earliest, most primitive; based on a 5-year (yuga) cycle; inferior in precision
2. **Vāsiṣṭha Siddhānta** — a 60-year cycle; primitive methods for sun, moon, and shadow lengths
3. **Romaka Siddhānta** — a 2,850-year luni-solar cycle; closely related to Hellenistic astronomy
4. **Pauliśa Siddhānta** — derived from a Greek ("Paulisa = Paulus") source; detailed planetary tables, sine function
5. **Saura Siddhānta** — the old Sūrya Siddhānta; most accurate; Varāhamihira's preferred system

Varāhamihira explicitly ranks the Saura as the best, then Romaka and Pauliśa as equally good, and dismisses the Vāsiṣṭha and Paitāmaha as inferior.

## Key Astronomical Highlights

- **Chapter 1:** Explicit ranking of siddhantas; epoch Śaka 427 = 505 CE
- **Chapter 5:** Romaka's 2,850-year luni-solar period — links to Metonic cycle and Hellenistic astronomy  
- **Chapter 6:** Pauliśa planetary revolution counts — evidence of Greek influence on Indian astronomy
- **Chapter 13:** *Trailokya-samsthāna* — geocentric cosmological model; Laṅkā on the equator; Meru at north pole
- **Chapter 18:** Heliacal visibility limits for all 7 classical planets; Varāhamihira's colophon
