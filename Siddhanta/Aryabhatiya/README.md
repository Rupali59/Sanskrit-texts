## About the Text

The *Aryabhatiya* is the foundational treatise of Indian mathematical astronomy, composed in 499 CE by the astronomer-mathematician Aryabhata at the age of 23. Written in terse verse (ārya metre), it packs an extraordinary amount of mathematics and astronomy into 121 shlokas across four chapters (*pāda*s).

Key contributions:
- **π ≈ 3.14159** (62832/20000), explicitly called *āsanna* (approximate) — the most accurate value of π in the world at the time
- **Earth's rotation**: the apparent westward movement of stars is caused by Earth's eastward rotation on its own axis (1,000 years before Copernicus)
- **Reflected moonlight**: the Moon and planets have no intrinsic light; they shine by reflected sunlight
- **Gravity**: the Earth attracts all objects toward its centre from all directions (proto-gravitational concept)
- **Kuttaka algorithm**: the earliest known systematic method for solving linear Diophantine equations (indeterminate equations) — equivalent to the extended Euclidean algorithm
- **Sine table**: 24 values of half-chords (sines) at 3°45' intervals, foundational for all subsequent Indian and Islamic trigonometry

## Structure

| Pāda | Chapter | Shlokas | Content |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Gitikapada** | 1 | 13 | Cosmological time constants: yugas, kalpas, planetary revolution counts |
| **Ganitapada** | 2 | 33 | Mathematics: square/cube roots, π, sine table, arithmetic series, geometry, kuttaka |
| **Kalakriyapada** | 3 | 25 | Reckoning of time: ahargaṇa, mean planets, eclipses, calendar constants |
| **Golapada** | 4 | 50 | Celestial sphere: Earth's shape & rotation, parallax, eclipse geometry, instruments |
| **Total** | | **121** | |
